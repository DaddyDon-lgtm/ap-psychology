The Normal Curve

Standard deviation, percentiles, and what the shape is telling you

1What a Distribution Is

A distribution is just a picture of how often each score occurred. Scores run along the bottom. Height shows how many people got that score. That is the whole idea, and everything else on this page is a description of the shape that picture makes.

The thing to notice

Set the standard deviation to 5, then to 30, with the bands showing. It is always 68% in the middle band. What changes is the range of scores that 68% covers. That is what standard deviation means.

3Standard Deviation in Plain Language

Standard deviation measures how far scores typically sit from the mean.

A small standard deviation means scores are bunched tightly around the mean and the group is fairly uniform. A large standard deviation means scores are spread widely and the group is diverse.

You will not be asked to calculate standard deviation on the AP exam. You will be asked to interpret it, compare two of them, and know what happens to the curve when it changes.

The rule worth knowing cold

In any normal distribution: about 68% of scores fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean, about 95% within 2, and about 99.7% within 3.

IQ scores have a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15. So about 68% of people score between 85 and 115, and about 95% score between 70 and 130. A score of 145 is three standard deviations above the mean, which fewer than 2 people in 1,000 reach.

The misreading, and it is extremely common

Percentile rank is not the percentage of questions you answered correctly. A student at the 90th percentile might have answered 60% of the items correctly. Those are two completely different numbers that both happen to be written as percents.

Percentile rank is the percentage of people you scored above. The median is always the 50th percentile, in any distribution.

5Skew and Two-Peaked Shapes

Slide from one skew to the other Interactive

Skew is named for the tail, not the hump

A positively skewed distribution has its tail stretching toward the high numbers, even though most of the scores are piled up low. A negatively skewed distribution has its tail stretching toward the low numbers.

This is backwards from how most students first guess it, so here it is again: look at where the thin tail points, not where the tall hump sits.

The mean is always the marker furthest out into the tail, because the mean is the only one of the three that feels distance.

ShapeReal examples
Positively skewedincome, reaction times, number of children per family
Negatively skewedscores on an easy test, age at retirement, lifespan

Bimodal distributions

A bimodal distribution has two peaks. It almost always means two different groups have been mixed together in one data set.

Measure the heights of everyone in a middle school and you get one smooth hump. Measure the heights of everyone in the building including staff and young children and you may get two humps, because you have combined populations that differ.

On the exam, a bimodal shape is a signal. It suggests the sample contains two distinct subgroups that should probably be analyzed separately. The mean of a bimodal distribution often falls in the valley between the two peaks, describing almost nobody. Switch the toggle above to see it.

6AP Exam Tips

  • Know 68, 95, 99.7 cold, and be able to apply it to a given mean and standard deviation without a calculator.
  • Percentile rank means the percentage scoring below you. It is never the percentage of items correct.
  • Skew is named for the tail. Positive skew means the tail points to the high scores.
  • In a skewed distribution, the mean is pulled furthest toward the tail, and the median is the better description of the typical score.
  • A bimodal distribution suggests two groups mixed together.
  • Standard deviation is about spread, never about center. A larger standard deviation does not mean higher scores.

7Practice Questions

Answer each one before you reveal it.

Question 1

A test has a mean of 500 and a standard deviation of 100.

Approximately what percentage of scores fall between 400 and 600?

  1. 34%
  2. 68%
  3. 95%
  4. 99.7%

Answer: B

400 is one standard deviation below the mean and 600 is one above, so this is the band within one SD either side, which holds about 68% of scores in a normal distribution.

  • A is the half: about 34% lies between the mean and one SD in one direction only.
  • C would be 300 to 700, which is two SD either side.
  • D would be 200 to 800, three SD either side.
Question 2

Maria scored at the 75th percentile on a standardized test.

What does this mean?

  1. She answered 75% of the questions correctly
  2. She scored higher than about 75% of test takers
  3. She scored 75 points above the mean
  4. Her score was 75% of the highest possible score

Answer: B

Percentile rank describes position relative to other people, not performance against the test content.

  • A is the most common misreading, and it is a completely different quantity. She could have answered half the items correctly and still placed at the 75th percentile if the test was hard.
  • C describes a raw score difference, which percentile does not report.
  • D describes a proportion of the maximum score, which percentile also does not report.
Question 3

A distribution of household incomes in a city has a mean of $74,000 and a median of $52,000.

Which statement is best supported?

  1. The distribution is negatively skewed
  2. The distribution is positively skewed, and the median better describes a typical household
  3. Most households earn about $74,000
  4. The distribution is normal

Answer: B

The mean sits well above the median, which happens when a tail of very high values pulls the mean up. That is positive skew. Because most households cluster below the mean, the median is the more honest description of a typical household, which is exactly why income is always reported as a median.

  • A has the direction reversed.
  • C is the error the mean invites here: very few households actually earn near $74,000.
  • D is wrong: in a normal distribution the mean and median would match.
Question 4

A teacher graphs her class results and finds two clear peaks, one around 55 and one around 85, with few scores in between.

What is the most reasonable interpretation?

  1. The test was too easy
  2. The data is positively skewed
  3. Two distinct groups are present in the class, and the mean would describe neither
  4. The standard deviation must be zero

Answer: C

Two peaks is a bimodal distribution, which usually means two subgroups have been combined. Here it might be students who had already covered the material and students who had not. The mean would land somewhere near 70, in the valley, describing almost no one.

  • A would produce a negatively skewed single peak bunched high.
  • B describes one peak with a high tail, not two peaks.
  • D is wrong: a standard deviation of zero would mean every score was identical.
